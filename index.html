Why preparation and honest matching matters

What happens when an AuPair
is not truly prepared, or truly matched

These are not rare exceptions. They happen every year across Germany. The AuPair programme is built on trust, and that trust breaks the moment the AuPair is not ready, or when the match was never real to begin with.

⚠ The child who choked
A 2-year-old choked on a piece of food. The AuPair had never been taught what to do. She panicked and called the parents instead of acting. The child survived. It was close. The AuPair had assured the family she had childcare experience. She did not.
⚠ Left alone with a newborn
An AuPair was left with a 6-week-old for the first time. She did not know how to hold the baby safely, did not know what to do when the baby would not stop crying, and had never prepared a bottle. She had said she was comfortable with babies during the interview.
⚠ The match that was never real
An AuPair applied to 40 families in one week. She accepted the first offer. She had never asked herself what she truly wanted from the year. The family had never asked themselves either. Within six weeks both sides were miserable, and the children were in the middle of it.
⚠ The AuPair who needed a bridge
She wanted Germany. Not the family, not the children. Germany. She was charming in the interview. She accepted the offer. She lasted four months before disappearing to a city where she had friends. The family was left without childcare and their children without someone they had grown attached to.

These children did nothing wrong. The system failed them because nobody prepared anyone, and nobody matched honestly.

Programme 1: The 5-day preparation

What you will actually learn and practise

Five days of real preparation. Practical, scenario-based. Not theory. You will leave knowing exactly what to do in the situations that define whether a placement succeeds or fails.

🏠 Day 1: The German household
How German families organise their homes. Safety standards in kitchen, bathroom, outdoor spaces. What children can reach at each age. Rules you will be expected to follow without being told twice.
🍽️ Day 2: Cooking for children
What German children eat, what they don't, how to prepare simple meals. Allergies, portion sizes, mealtimes, what to do when a child refuses to eat. The kitchen is where most AuPairs struggle first.
👶 Day 3: Childcare in practice
Age-appropriate activities, supervision levels, sleep routines, nappy changes, screen time, outdoor play. The difference between managing and truly caring for a child.
💬 Day 4: Communication in a German home
How to communicate clearly with German parents who are direct and have high expectations. How to raise a problem without causing conflict. How to ask for help without appearing incompetent.
⚠️ Day 5: Emergencies and certification
What to do in every type of emergency , medical, accident, child distress. First aid basics. Emergency contacts. Final assessment and the Academy AuPair certificate.

⚠ She wanted Germany , not your family
She applied to your family to get a visa and live in Germany. She was not dishonest in a cruel way , she had simply never been asked to think clearly about what she truly wanted. She left after four months. Your children had grown attached to her. You were left without childcare and with the damage of an abrupt goodbye they did not understand.
⚠ The placement that destroyed family life
A family welcomed an AuPair who arrived overwhelmed and completely unprepared. She could not manage the children, could not communicate clearly, and broke down within three weeks. The family spent two months managing the crisis while also managing the children. Their youngest had nightmares for months after the AuPair left suddenly.

You deserve an AuPair who arrives knowing what to do, and who genuinely chose your family for the right reasons.

What is Ausbildung?

Germany's most powerful pathway ,
and most AuPairs miss it

Ausbildung is Germany's vocational training system , a government-recognised paid programme where you train on the job in a company while attending vocational school. At the end, you hold a German qualification that opens doors for life.

€900–1,150
Monthly training pay
2–3 years
Duration + qualification
Legal visa
Path to stay long-term
You already have an advantage
You are already in Germany. You understand the culture. You speak some German. You have a German reference. Employers see this , and it matters more than you think.
Timing is everything
Most Ausbildung programmes start in September or February. If you are in your AuPair year now, the window to apply is open. Miss it and you wait another year , or go home.

The gap most companies don’t name

The person you hired is capable. The friction is cultural , and it is fixable.

Your new team member from India is qualified. She or he wants to do well. And yet , communication feels difficult. Feedback lands differently than intended. Expectations on both sides go unspoken until something goes wrong.

This is not a skills problem. It is a cultural preparation problem. And it exists on both sides: the international hire was not prepared for German workplace culture, and the German team was not prepared for them.

Goodwill on both sides is not enough. Preparation is what turns goodwill into a working relationship.
